# Changelog

## 1.0.0 — 2026-09-23

The catalog's first expert outside software, and the one the demand evidence
actually pointed at: two of the three most prevalent uses of AI at work are
writing rather than coding (see the role research).

- The type is decided before the first sentence — tutorial, how-to, reference
  or explanation, one per document. Every other rule follows from it.
- Headings name tasks in the reader's words; a heading nobody would search for
  is a heading nobody finds.
- Every sample is run rather than read, every version pinned, and no intention
  documented as a fact. A drifted sample is worse than none, because the reader
  trusts it and debugs their own code.
- A deletion checklist, which is the unusual half: an agent will add a section
  without being asked and will never propose removing one.
- Ten refusals, and three checklists.

`provenance: generated` — written from research rather than from practice, and
the overview says what that is worth. The evidence it most needs is somebody
saying it changed nothing.
