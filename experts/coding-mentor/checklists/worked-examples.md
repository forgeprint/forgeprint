# Worked examples

A new idea starts from a complete example and hands the steps over one at a
time. Read against the transcript or the tutorial.

| #   | Check                                                                                            | How to find it                                                             | Source                                                                                     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| WE1 | A concept new to a beginner is introduced with a complete worked example, explained step by step | the first appearance of the concept is a full example, not a problem       | Sweller and Cooper, _The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving_, 1985 |
| WE2 | The next examples are faded: steps removed for the learner to complete                           | later examples contain blanks or "your turn" steps                         | Renkl, Atkinson, Maier and Staley, 2002                                                    |
| WE3 | Fading removes the last steps first                                                              | the first faded example leaves the final step open                         | Renkl et al. 2002 (backward fading outperformed forward fading)                            |
| WE4 | Fading ends with a full problem the learner solves unaided                                       | the sequence ends in a problem with no worked steps                        | Renkl et al. 2002                                                                          |
| WE5 | An intermediate learner who shows the skill gets problems, not further full examples             | after a correct unaided solution, no new full example for the same concept | Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler and Sweller, 2003                                                 |
| WE6 | The example is shorter than the learner's attention: one idea per example                        | count the concepts introduced in one example; more than one is a finding   | Sweller and Cooper 1985 (cognitive load of problem-solving search)                         |

## Why each one

**WE1** is the counter-intuitive one. An agent's instinct is to set a problem
and help; Sweller and Cooper found that novices learned faster and with fewer
errors by studying worked examples than by solving the same problems.

**WE3** is the specific finding worth remembering: when steps are handed over,
the last ones go first.

**WE5** keeps the method from backfiring. Guidance that helps a novice becomes
redundant, and harmful, for somebody who already has the skill.
