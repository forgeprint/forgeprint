# Which roles outside software, and on what evidence

_Run 2026-09-23. Companion to [the demand report](2026-09-23-demand.md), which
covers blueprints. This one covers experts._

The taxonomy holds 58 roles across 16 domains and the catalog fills 5 of them
(ADR 0012). The list is deliberately wider than the content, so the question
this report answers is not "which roles exist" but **which empty ones have
evidence behind them**, as opposed to being interesting.

## The rule this report exists to enforce

An expert written from research rather than from practice is
`provenance: generated`, says so, and cannot be `tier: official` (ADR 0011).
That is the honest floor. The bar above it is this: **an expert is worth
writing when somebody is already asking an agent to do that job and getting an
unopinionated answer.** Not when the role is important.

Without that evidence the catalog fills with roles somebody thought sounded
good, which is the junk drawer this project's positioning is against.

## What the evidence says

| Finding                                                          | Number                                                        | Source, date                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Professional developers using AI at work                         | 90%                                                           | [DORA, State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025](https://dora.dev/dora-report-2025/), read 2026-09-23 |
| Relying on AI for at least one daily professional responsibility | more than 75%                                                 | DORA 2025, read 2026-09-23                                                                                  |
| The most prevalent uses named                                    | code writing, **information summarisation**, code explanation | DORA 2025, read 2026-09-23                                                                                  |
| Developers who write tests and use AI for quality tasks          | 62%                                                           | DORA 2025, read 2026-09-23                                                                                  |
| Reporting little or no trust in AI-generated code                | 30%                                                           | DORA 2025, read 2026-09-23                                                                                  |

One number that is widely repeated and that this report does **not** rely on:
several secondary sources attribute "64% use AI for writing documentation" to
DORA 2025. It could not be confirmed on dora.dev itself at the reading above,
so it is recorded here as unverified rather than cited as fact. The direction
it points is already carried by "information summarisation" being one of the
three most prevalent uses.

## What follows for the catalog

Two of the top three named uses — summarisation and explanation — are not
coding. They are writing. That is the strongest non-software signal available,
and it is what this round acts on:

| Role               | Domain  | Why now                                                                                     | Why it can be checkable                                                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `technical-writer` | docs    | Summarisation and explanation are two of the three most prevalent uses of AI at work        | Every document has a type, every claim a source, every sample runs. All checkable                                           |
| `product-manager`  | product | The other half of what gets summarised: requirements, decisions and what is not being built | Acceptance criteria, a metric per decision, and an explicit "not doing" list are checkable; opinions about strategy are not |

## What this round deliberately does not do

- **The other nine empty domains.** Finance, legal, education, marketing,
  operations, support, research, AI and design have no evidence in the numbers
  above beyond being jobs people have. Writing experts for them now would be
  taste wearing a citation.
- **`official` for anything here.** Nobody on this repository can verify a
  legal or financial expert by hand, and the tier that says a person stands
  behind it is not available to work nobody stood behind (ADR 0011, and the
  scope limit this expansion set for itself).
- **A role because it is prestigious.** `cloud-architect` and `ml-engineer`
  would both look good in the catalog and neither has a demand signal that
  distinguishes it from the architect already there.

## The mechanism, so the next round is not taste either

The site publishes every role with no expert behind it — 53 of them today —
and each is a contribution call. Two signals will drive the next round, and
both are public:

1. **Requested experts.** The open-roles list and the issues people open
   against it. Demand stated by the person who has it beats demand inferred
   from a survey.
2. **This report, re-run.** `skills/catalog-research` now covers roles as well
   as blueprints, so the next run answers the same question with newer numbers.

**Next run due 2026-10-23**, alongside the blueprint demand report.
