# Which expert roles coding-agent users reach for, and on what evidence

_Run 2026-09-24. Follows [the 2026-09-23 role report](2026-09-23-roles.md),
which chose the first two experts outside software. This round asks the wider
question: across the whole taxonomy, which roles do people using coding agents
(Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Copilot, Gemini CLI) actually install, download
or ask for?_

The rule from the previous report still holds: **an expert is worth writing
when somebody is already asking an agent to do that job**, and it must be
checkable, meaning every deliverable and checklist item cites a named standard.
A role with demand but no standard is refused. A role with a standard but no
demand waits.

## How the numbers were taken

Three kinds of evidence were used. None of them measures usage directly, and
each has a trap.

## Decision recorded: experts multiply per stack

The maintainer has decided that experts may be multiplied per language or
stack, the way `dotnet-senior-architect` already is. A `node-senior-architect`
or a `java-senior-architect` is therefore a legitimate second architect, not a
duplicate.

**Follow-up:** that collides with rule 9, which today makes
`role + domain + seniority` unique per expert. The collision is resolved by
**widening rule 9 through an ADR** so that the stack is part of an expert's
identity. It is **not** resolved by giving a second architect a different
seniority: seniority describes how the expert works, and bending it to dodge a
uniqueness check would make the field meaningless. Until that ADR lands, no
second expert may be added for a role, domain and seniority that is already
taken.

## What this round deliberately did not do

- **Write any expert.** This is evidence, not content. Every "New" row is a
  candidate, and each still needs its own pull request.
- **Extend the taxonomy.** The three gaps above are recorded here; each is a
  separate taxonomy pull request, as the taxonomy rules require.
- **Rank by stars.** Stars are listed nowhere in the ranking. They measure
  attention, and the collections with the most stars are not the ones whose
  individual agents show the most downloads.
- **Treat bundled installs as demand.** Where a skill's count is its whole
